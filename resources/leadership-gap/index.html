No Director of Technology? Options, risks and questions for your cabinet

When no one owns the big picture, technology decisions land on whoever opens the email: the business manager, a principal, the superintendent or an already stretched IT team. Here is how to see who owns what today, compare four ways to cover the seat, and bring one clear decision to cabinet.

Section 1

What the seat actually owns

The title matters less than the decisions. CoSN puts it plainly: "technology touches virtually everything in a district" (CoSN, 2026 [S1]). These eight duties need a named owner whether or not you fill the role.

  1. Cybersecurity priorities and incident decisionsWhat gets fixed first, and who decides in the first hour of an incident.
  2. Renewal and contract calendarEvery license and service agreement, its renewal date and who can stop it.
  3. Student data privacy agreementsWhich apps hold student data and whether a signed agreement is on file.
  4. E-Rate and other filing deadlinesForms, windows and documentation that cannot be filed late.
  5. Multi-year technology plan and device refreshWhat gets replaced when, and how it fits the budget cycle.
  6. Board and cabinet technology reportingPlain-language updates on risks, projects and decisions needed.
  7. Vendor managementWho talks to vendors, checks their work and holds them to the contract.
  8. Staff support prioritiesWhat the help desk and IT staff work on first, and what waits.

A

Hire full-time

or an interim while you recruit

Best when
You can recruit and keep a technology leader and want someone on site every day.
Strengths
Full-time attention, on-site presence, deep knowledge of your buildings and people.
Trade-offs and risks
Hard to recruit in a competitive market. One person cannot cover every specialty. The vacancy problem returns if they leave.
More: time to start, continuity, board voice, cyber, renewals, staff load
Time to get started
Slowest. Posting, interviews, board approval and notice periods all take time. An interim can bridge the gap.
Continuity
Strong once the person is in place. At risk if they leave, because knowledge often sits with one person.
Cabinet and board voice
Strong, if the role is placed on the superintendent's cabinet.
Cybersecurity ownership
Clear. One named owner on staff.
Renewal and vendor control
Strong. Daily presence and direct relationships with vendors.
Load on existing staff
Low once hired. High during the vacancy and onboarding.
Ask before choosing
Can we fill and keep this role? Who covers each duty until the person starts, and how will their knowledge be written down?
B

Share a director

with a neighboring district or through your regional service agency

Best when
A neighbor or your regional agency (intermediate unit, BOCES, ESC or similar) already offers shared leadership and your needs are similar.
Strengths
Shared expertise, regional relationships, common standards and purchasing across districts.
Trade-offs and risks
Priorities compete when both districts need help at once. Governance is more complex. Less say in who is assigned.
More: time to start, continuity, board voice, cyber, renewals, staff load
Time to get started
Depends on agreements between boards and on the agency's capacity.
Continuity
Good with a multi-year agreement. Depends on the partner's or agency's staffing.
Cabinet and board voice
Shared. The director may attend some of your cabinet and board meetings, not all.
Cybersecurity ownership
Shared. Needs a written answer to who gets priority if an incident hits more than one district.
Renewal and vendor control
Possible joint purchasing benefits, but your renewal calendar competes with your partners'.
Load on existing staff
Moderate. Local staff still make many day-to-day decisions.
Ask before choosing
Who gets priority in a shared incident? How are decisions split between districts? What happens if one district leaves the arrangement?
C

Fractional or contracted director

a part-time technology leader on a regular schedule

Best when
You need senior planning, vendor and board-level leadership on a regular schedule, and you have staff or a managed service provider for daily support.
Strengths
Senior experience and an outside view, with a part-time commitment that matches part-time needs.
Trade-offs and risks
Not on site every day. Depends on a good working rhythm with staff. Contract scope must be clear about what is and is not included. The district still needs named internal decision-makers.
More: time to start, continuity, board voice, cyber, renewals, staff load
Time to get started
Usually quicker than recruiting, subject to your procurement rules and board approval.
Continuity
Good with a multi-year arrangement and written documentation the district keeps. Changes if the contract ends.
Cabinet and board voice
Can attend cabinet and present to the board on a set schedule, but that schedule has to be planned on purpose.
Cybersecurity ownership
Can own priorities, planning and the incident decision structure. Hands-on response still relies on local staff or vendors.
Renewal and vendor control
Strong, if the renewal calendar and vendor reviews are written into the scope.
Load on existing staff
Moderate. Staff still need time for regular check-ins and follow-through.
Ask before choosing
What exactly is in scope? How often on site, and how quickly do they respond during an incident? Who owns the documentation if the contract ends?

Section 4

Five questions for your next cabinet meeting

Each one has a short, specific answer. If the room goes quiet, you have found a gap.

  1. If a vendor contract auto-renews next month, who has the authority to stop it, and do they know the date?Look for a name and a calendar, not "the business office will catch it."
  2. Who would make the first decisions in the first hour of a cyber incident, and is that written down where staff can find it?Include who calls the insurer, who talks to families and who can take systems offline.
  3. Who presents technology risks to the board, and when did that last happen?If the answer is "only when something breaks," that is the gap.
  4. When a teacher wants a new app that handles student data, who approves it?One person or role, known to every building.
  5. Which technology deadline before the end of this school year has no named owner?Check E-Rate, renewals, device orders and the technology plan.

The takeaway

One decision for your cabinet

Name an owner for each of the eight duties within 30 days. Write the names down and share them with every building.

Then choose how to cover the seat for the longer term, using the comparison above. Naming owners first makes any option easier to judge, and safer while you decide.
